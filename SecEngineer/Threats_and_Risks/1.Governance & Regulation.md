# Governance & Regulation

## Intro

As cyber threats evolve, **strong information security governance and regulation are essential**. Organizations must set clear security policies, assign responsibility for oversight, and continuously monitor and enforce compliance. Done well, this reduces exposure to attacks and protects sensitive systems and data from high-impact incidents.

## Why is it Important?

### Important Terms

- **Governance**: *Managing and directing an organization or system* to achieve its objectives and ensure compliance with laws, regulations, and standards.
- **Regulation**: A rule or law enforced by a governing body to ensure compliance and protect against harm.
- **Compliance**: The state of adhering to laws, regulations, and standards that apply to an organization or system.

### Information Security Governance

Represents an organization's established structure, policies, methods, and guidelines designed to guarantee the privacy, reliability, and accessibility of its information assets.

It falls within the purview of top-tier management, it includes the following processes (and it is essential to them):

- **Strategy**: Developing and implementing a comprehensive information security strategy that aligns with the organization's overall *business objectives*.
- **Policies and Procedures**: Preparing policies and procedures that govern the use and protection of information assets.
- **Risk Management**: Conduct risk assessments to identify potential threats to the organization assets and implement risk mitigation measures.
- **Performance Measurement**: Establishing metrics and key performance indicators (KPIs) to measure the effectiveness of the information security governance program.
- **Compliance**: Ensuring compliance.

## Information Security Frameworks

They provide a comprehensive set of documents that outline the organization's approach to information security and governs how security is implemented, managed, and enforced within the organization. This mainly includes:

- Policies: A formal statement that outlines an organisation's goals, principles, and guidelines for achieving specific objectives.
- Standards: A document establishing specific requirements or specifications for a particular process, product, or service.
- Guidelines:  A document that provides recommendations and best practices (non-mandatory) for achieving specific goals or objectives.
- Procedures: Set of specific steps for undertaking a particular task or process.
- Baselines: A set of minimum security standards or requirements that an organisation or system must meet.

### Developing Governance Documents

- **Identify the scope and purpose**: What we will cover with the document and why is it needed?
- **Research and Review**: The document needs to be comprehensive and up to date. For this, we research (relevant laws, regulations, industry standards, best practices) and then review other governance documents (policies, procedures, etc) to avoid duplicated efforts or contradicting exiting guidance.
- **Draft the document**: To make an outline of the document follow best practices (makes it clear and concise). Ensure the doc is specific and actionable.
- **Review and approval**: Have the document reviewed by stakeholders (subject matter experts, legal and compliance teams, and senior management) and incorporate their feedback. Then, obtain their approval...
- **Implementation and communication**: Communicate the document to all relevant employees and stakeholders, and ensure they understand their roles and responsibilities in implementing it. To ensure understanding and compliance, develop training and awareness programs.
- **Review and update**: Periodically review and update the doc to ensure it remains relevant and practical. Monitor compliance and adjust the document based on feedback and changes (in the threat landscape or regulatory environment).

> Governance documents must be developed considering alignment with organizational goals and values.

## Governance Risk and Compliance (GRC)

How do we maintain an organization's security posture? Through the GRC framework, which focuses, on an integrated manner, on steering the organization's:

- **Governance Component**: Guiding an organization by setting its direction through information security strategy (including policies, standards, baselines, etc) along with establishing appropriate monitoring methods to measure performance and assess the outcomes.
- **Risk Management Component**: Managing risks involves identifying, assessing and prioritizing risks to the organization and implementing controls and mitigation strategies to manage those risks effectively (along with monitoring and reporting risks, and continuously refining the risk management program).
- **Compliance Component**: Ensuring that the organization meets its legal, regulatory, and industry obligations and that is activities align with its policies and procedures (how? well: developing and implementing compliance programs, conducting regular audits and assessments, and reporting on compliance to stakeholders).

## Privacy and Data Protection

Privacy and data protection regulations are critical as they deal with citizens' PII (In every sector: financial, healthcare, government, and industry). 

Privacy regulations help:

- Ensure individuals' personal information is handled and stored responsibly and ethically.
- To stablish trust, protect personal information and maintain regulatory compliance.

These are some important privacy and data protection regulations.

### GDPR

Protection law implemented by the EU to protect PII.

Key points include:

- Prior approval must be obtained before collecting any personal data.
- Personal data should be kept to a minimum and only collected when necessary.
- Adequate measures are to be adopted to protect stored personal data.

The law applies to all business entities that conduct business in the European Union (EU) and collect/store/process the personal data of EU residents and are required to comply.

This also includes penalties and fines based on non-compliance in the following two tiers:

- **Tier 1**: More severe violations, including unintended data collection, sharing data with third parties without consent, etc. Maximum penalty amounting to 4% of the organization's revenue or 20 million euros (whichever is higher).
- **Tier 2**:  Less severe violations, including data breach notifications, cyber policies, etc. The maximum fine for Tier 2 is 2% of the organization's revenue or 10 million euros (whichever is higher).

### PCI DSS

PCI DSS is focused on maintaining secure card transactions and protecting against data theft and fraud. It is widely used by businesses, primarily online, for card-based transactions. It was established by major credit card brands (Visa, MasterCard & American Express). It requires strict control access to cardholder information and monitoring unauthorised access, using recommended measures such as web application firewalls and encryption.

## NIST Special Publications

### NIST 800-53

A publication titled "Security and Privacy Controls for Information Systems and Organisations", developed by NIST that provides a catalogue of **security controls** to protect the CIA triad of information systems. NIST 800-53 serves as a framework for organizations to assess and enhance the security and privacy of their information systems and comply with various laws, regulations, and policies. It incorporates best practices from multiple sources.

NIST organizes security controls into 20 families, each family addresses a specific security concern category (out of the 5):

| Configuration Management (CM) | System and Communication Protection (SC) | Media Protection (MP) | |
| Contingency Planning (CP) | System and Information Integrity (SI) | |
| Incident Response (IR) | System and Service Acquisition (SA) | |
| Maintenance (MA) | System and Services Development (SD) | |
| Program Management (PM) | Program System and Services Development (DS) | |

Among all the families, "Program Management" is one of the crucial control of the NIST 800-53 framework. **Program Management ensures that** an organization defines, runs, and oversees security and privacy programs at an enterprise level. Its goal is to make sure information security and privacy are consistently planned, implemented, and monitored across all systems, protecting data whether it is processed, stored, or transmitted.

### NIST 800-63B

NIST Special Publication 800-63B is a set of guidelines created by the NIST to help organizations establish effective digital identity practices. Its primary focus is on authenticating and verifying the identities of individuals who access digital services, systems, and networks.

## Information Security Management and Compliance

Information Security (IS) Management focuses on **planning, implementing, and continuously managing security controls** to protect information from unauthorised access, misuse, disclosure, disruption, modification, or destruction. It includes **risk assessment, control development, incident response, and security awareness training**.
**Compliance** ensures the organisation follows **legal, regulatory, contractual, and industry security requirements**.

Two widely used standards in IS management and compliance are **ISO/IEC 27001** and **SOC 2**.

### ISO/IEC 27001

ISO 27001 is an international standard for establishing and maintaining an **Information Security Management System (ISMS)**. It defines how organisations systematically manage information security risks.

Key components include:

* **Scope:** Defines the boundaries of the ISMS.
* **Information security policy:** High-level security direction.
* **Risk assessment:** Identifies risks to confidentiality, integrity, and availability.
* **Risk treatment:** Selects controls to reduce risks.
* **Statement of Applicability (SoA):** Lists applicable and excluded controls.
* **Internal audit:** Evaluates ISMS effectiveness.
* **Management review:** Periodic review of ISMS performance.

**Benefits of ISO 27001**

* Provides a structured, risk-based approach to security.
* Establishes clear policies, processes, and controls.
* Requires leadership involvement and resource support.
* Emphasises continuous monitoring and improvement.
* Aligns security practices with organisational objectives.

### Service Organization Control 2 (SOC 2)

SOC 2 is a **compliance and auditing framework** developed by the AICPA. It evaluates how well service organisations protect data based on the **CIA principles (Confidentiality, Integrity, Availability)** and privacy.

SOC 2 is especially important for **service providers handling customer data**, such as cloud and financial service companies, and is often requested by customers as proof of strong security practices.

**Key points about SOC 2**

* An **independent audit** assesses the effectiveness of security controls.
* Covers areas such as physical security, network security, encryption, backup, and staff training.
* Audit reports provide assurance to customers, regulators, and partners.
* Findings may include gaps and recommendations for improvement.

### SOC 2 Audit Process

Typical steps include:

* Define the audit scope.
* Select a qualified auditor.
* Plan the audit timeline and criteria.
* Review and improve existing controls.
* Undergo control testing and evaluation.
* Receive an audit report with results and recommendations.

**SOC 2 Protection Focus**

SOC 2 ensures that third-party service providers **securely store and process sensitive information**, demonstrating accountability and trustworthiness.
